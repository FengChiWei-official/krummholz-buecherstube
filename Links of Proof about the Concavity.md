## Definition

Two typical proofs about concavity:
1. [[Secant Integral Inequality by Midpoint Expansion]] — the integral form.
2. [[Secant]] → [[Jensen Inequality]] — the pointwise form.

## Perspectives

Some tricks, all from [[Taylor Series]]:
- Endpoint expansion → Tangent line scaling
- Expansion at the midpoint → Secant line scaling

> [!note]
> [[Expansion in the Integral]] is a useful skill.

## Distinctions

- (1) bounds an *average* — an integral — by the value at the midpoint; (2) bounds a *value* at a weighted midpoint by the values at the two ends.
- (1) needs $f'' \ge 0$ on the interval; (2) needs convexity, which is the same requirement written pointwise.

## Examples

- [[Secant Integral Inequality by Midpoint Expansion]]: $f$ with $f'' \ge 0$ on $[a,b]$.
- [[Jensen Inequality]]: $f$ convex, weights $a, b \ge 0$.
